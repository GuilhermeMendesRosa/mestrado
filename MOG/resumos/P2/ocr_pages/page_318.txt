10.4 Generalized Notation Scheme and
Higher-Dimension Elements

To introduce each subject, the preceding sections used a traditional algebraic
notation scheme. Incorporating vector and matrix notation significantly reduces the
complexity and awkwardness of these formulations. The next logical step is to intro-
duce a more generalized summation scheme that extends to the representation of not
only curves and surfaces, but also to solids and higher-order geometric forms. The
notation convention we use here (largely attributable to Timmer) has these charac-
teristics and others. It can extend to spaces of any dimension; it applies to polycubic
interpolation involving an arbitrary number of independent parametric variables and
suggests a system that can directly extend to any odd-ordered polynomial form.
